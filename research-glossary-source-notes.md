# Research Glossary Source Notes

These notes support the private Research Overview glossary. The on-page definitions must remain concise, laboratory-focused, and free of dosing, administration, personal-use, treatment, prevention, safety, or outcome guidance.

## Authoritative sources

1. **NIH / PubMed Central — “Second Messengers”**  
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4968160/  
   Source principle: second messengers are small molecules or ions that relay signals received by cell-surface receptors to target proteins. The article identifies cAMP as a classic second messenger.

2. **IUPAC — Glossary of Terms Used in Medicinal Chemistry**  
   https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/medchem/ix.html  
   Source principles: a receptor is a cellular molecule or structure that specifically recognizes and binds a molecular messenger; a second messenger is an intracellular metabolite or ion that changes in response to receptor stimulation; a structure–activity relationship connects chemical structure with measured activity across a series of compounds.

## Proposed plain-language research definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Receptor | A molecule on or in a cell that recognizes and binds a specific signaling molecule. |
| Receptor agonist | A research compound used to activate a receptor response in an experimental model. |
| Multi-receptor / triple-agonist | A research design intended to engage more than one receptor pathway in the same experimental model. |
| Receptor pharmacology | Laboratory study of how research compounds bind to and influence receptor signaling. |
| cAMP | A small intracellular messenger researchers measure to compare how receptor signals are relayed inside cells. |
| Second-messenger assay | A laboratory test that measures intracellular signaling molecules produced after receptor activation. |
| Structure–activity relationship | Comparison of how changes in molecular structure relate to measured activity in a research series. |
| Metabolic signaling | Laboratory study of signaling pathways involved in how experimental systems process and regulate energy-related inputs. |
| Lipid handling | Laboratory study of how experimental cells or tissues process, transport, store, or use lipids. |
| Energy-regulation pathway | A signaling network studied for its role in how experimental systems manage energy-related processes. |

## Interface boundary

The glossary should be labeled as plain-language **research terminology**. Definitions explain terms already present in product copy; they do not add product claims, human-use instructions, medical advice, or personal-use guidance.
